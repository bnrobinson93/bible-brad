In [[John]] 9, Jesus heals a man born blind. He spits in the dirt, making mud, and rubs this mud onto a blind beggar's eyes. He then tells him to go wash. Upon washing, the man's eyes are opened and he sees (this is the man who's words inspired the song "Amazing Grace"). 

The [[Pharisees]], learning that, not only had he been healed on a [[Sabbath]], [[Jesus]] had also made mud and placed saliva in someone's eyes (which is forbidden by the [[rabbis]][^1]). They respond with disbelief, name-calling, and piousness. [[Jesus]], when he hears of this, states plainly that he came to judge the world. That judgement would come in the form of healing those who thought they were blind and showing those who think they can see are actually the blind ones. The [[Pharisees]] claimed to have special insight into what God cared about but they were lost and hopeless, clinging to power and authority, not God's heart.

[^1]: Saliva of the first born was considered to have healing abilities, particular for blindness. Healing in any form - including medicine - was forbidden on Sabbath. Making mud was also forbidden, as it could be used for bricks. Also, asking the man to bathe was questionable among a sunset of leaders.
